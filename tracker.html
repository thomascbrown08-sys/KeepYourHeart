Recording Template

Something to fill in and hand across, so the session does not start with “how was your week?” and end forty minutes later still trying to answer.

Print this. Fill it in by hand, badly, in about ten minutes. Bring it.

Read this before you use it — it matters more than the form

This is not a grade and there is no score. Chapter two contains a case study about a woman whose immaculate, colour-coded practice log had quietly become the instrument she was using to hold her own trial. That is a real pattern, it is common among exactly the sort of conscientious person who fills in forms carefully, and this page could become it.

So: blanks are fine. A week where you filled in two boxes is not a failed week. If you find yourself dreading this page, or tidying it before handing it over, or feeling worse after completing it — stop using it and say so at your next session. The dread is more useful information than anything you would have written.

Week beginning

1 · The floor

Chapter eleven. Answer these before concluding anything about your spiritual or emotional state, because the answers change what the rest of the page means.

2 · What actually happened

Facts only. No adjectives about yourself. One or two events, not the whole week.

3 · Sorting it

Chapter three. Expect more than one column to have something in it. Notice which column you throw everything into.

4 · Reading the instrument

Chapter thirteen. Two different questions; do not answer one when you were asked the other.

5 · Other people

6 · Practice

Not a tally. Just what you actually did, if anything.

7 · What I want from this session

The most important box on the page. If you fill in nothing else, fill in this.

For a counselor

This form is structured around the book’s own categories, so the client’s answers will use its vocabulary. Three things may be worth your attention.

Section 1 comes first deliberately. Religious clients often under-report physical factors and over-attribute to spiritual or moral causes; putting the floor at the top is an attempt to interrupt that before interpretation begins.

Section 3 is the most diagnostic. Which column a client fills reflexively — and which they leave blank — tends to say more than the contents. A client who puts everything under iniquity is doing something quite different from one who puts everything under injury.

The form itself can become a symptom. Meticulous completion, distress at gaps, or tidying before handing it over are worth naming directly; the page warns the client about exactly this, so you can raise it as something they were already told to watch for rather than as a criticism.
