A Workbook and a Short Textbook

Keep Your Heart

Christian foundations for the work of emotional health — written for people in therapy, for the friends who love them, and for the counselors who sit across from them.

Sooner or later, most people who take a skills class in emotional regulation run into a snag. The skills work. Something underneath them does not sit right.

This book is written for that moment. It takes the problems that Dialectical Behavior Therapy was built to solve — emotions that arrive at the wrong size, distress that feels unsurvivable, relationships that keep detonating — and works them from the ground up out of Scripture and the long Christian tradition of soul care.

It is not a debunking. Much of what DBT observes about human beings is simply true, and true things are God’s property no matter who found them. What this book does is put those observations on a different foundation, run them on a different engine, and point them at a different destination.

Before you begin

This book is not treatment and is not a replacement for it. DBT was developed for people in serious danger, and its crisis material exists to keep people alive on their worst night. Nothing here replaces that. Where a chapter approaches crisis territory, it will say so and hand you back to your clinician.

If you are in immediate danger, contact your clinician now, or in the US call or text 988.

Where to start

If you are the person struggling, read the Note to the Reader first. If you are a counselor who has been handed this by a client, the Note to the Counselor is one page and will tell you what you need to know.

And if you are tired — too tired to start a book — go to Nutshells. It is the whole argument in single sentences, gathered from the chapters themselves, and you can read it in four minutes.

Contents

Front Matter

  1. —Note to the Reader What this is, what it is not, and who wrote it.
  2. —Note to the Counselor One page, plain language, for a therapist who may not share the framework.

Part I · Foundations

  1. 1All Truth Is God’s Truth Why a Christian can use a skill developed outside the faith without swallowing the worldview that came with it.
  2. 2The Engine Problem Two people can do identical work. One is free and one is dying. The difference is what the work is for.
  3. 3Why You Hurt A sin you did, a weakness you carry, a wound that was done to you. Three different things, and handing someone the wrong one is cruel.
  4. 4Both/And Without Losing the Floor The Christian answer to dialectics — more paradox, not less, but with somewhere to stand.

Part II · Attending

  1. 5Before the Face of God Self-observation is only restful if the observer is kind. Yours is made of the same weather as the rest of you.
  2. 6Talk to Yourself Your self talks to you every morning without being asked. Psalm 42 says you are permitted to interrupt.
  3. 7Filled, Not Emptied You already know how to meditate. Depression taught you, and taught you well. It just chose the material.
  4. 8Examined but Not Absorbed Looking inward is safe in exactly the proportion that the verdict is not at stake in it.

Part III · Enduring

  1. 9The Cup and the Garden He did not accept the cup because it was unavoidable. He said out loud that it was avoidable.
  2. 10Lament God wrote a hymnbook and made complaint the largest section in it.
  3. 11Bread and Sleep and Sabbath God’s first treatment for a prophet who wanted to die was a meal and a nap. Twice, before any conversation.
  4. 12The Key of Promise The key was in his pocket the whole time — an exact description of what despair does to memory.

Part IV · Loving

  1. 13Ordered Affections An emotion is not noise to be silenced. It is a reading — and the question is what it is a reading of.
  2. 14Anger The one emotion the book has not yet taken on directly, and the easiest thing in the world to counterfeit from inside.
  3. 15Truth in Love Every communication technique assumes you can survive being disliked. For some of us that is the whole problem.
  4. 16Repair How an apology is actually made — and why the hardest part is stopping.
  5. 17Burdens and Loads Carrying what was never yours and refusing help with what is. These look like opposite faults. They are one.

Part V · Closing

  1. 18A Life You Did Not Build An inheritance is not something you build. It is something somebody died to leave you.

Part VI · The Bruised Reed

A second door, for readers carrying a history the ordinary counsel does not fit. Each chapter stands alone.

  1. —The Bruised Reed Who this part is for, the two guards on it, and where the name comes from.
  2. 19The Room With No Cost A room where only one person is exposed is not a lit room. It is an examination.
  3. 20Searched, Not Surveilled You cannot be found out by someone who already knows. Telling him is not a transfer of information.
  4. 21Care That Was Not Care Care is glad when you no longer need it. Control is threatened by the same fact.
  5. 22Church Hurt Harm done in God’s name, where the injury and the place you would go for help are the same building.
  6. 23Formed and Answerable Say it as two sentences. In one, the second sounds like a qualification of the first.
  7. 24Guilt That Repairs, Shame That Consumes Two men betrayed him in the same week. Both grieved. Only one of them survived it.
  8. 25What Was Done To You Forgiveness, reconciliation and trust are three different things. Most of the damage comes from being handed all three as one.
  9. 26A Name They Did Not Give You The label may be accurate. Accurate and total are different words, and the whole fight is the gap between them.
  10. 27The Record That Survives You are not the only person who knows. You are wrong about that by exactly one.
