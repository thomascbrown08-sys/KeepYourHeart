Judgment-Call Log

Every place where a decision could reasonably have gone the other way.

A book that asks you to trust it about your interior life owes you an account of where it made choices.

This page exists mainly for reviewers — a pastor, a theologian, a reader who disagrees — but any reader is welcome in it. Each entry names the call, says which way it went, and gives the reason. Nothing here is hidden elsewhere in the book; every one of these is flagged in the chapter where it happens. This page just gathers them so they can be argued with in one sitting.

Posture toward DBT

Naming the treatment explicitly

Named, and treated as a serious interlocutor

The alternative was to work at the level of “the problems it addresses” and never name it. Named was chosen because arguing with an unnamed opponent is cowardly and because the reader is in the room with it weekly. No skills lists, acronyms, worksheets, or training sequence are reproduced anywhere; those belong to their authors.

Four separate verdicts rather than one

Observations excellent, explanation incomplete, engine underpowered, destination too small

Chapter one splits any account of human beings into four layers and insists the verdicts stay separate. A reviewer who thinks the book is too warm toward DBT and one who thinks it is too critical are usually reacting to different layers.

The mindfulness critique framed as our shortage, not their infiltration

Framed as a bill against us

Chapter one argues that DBT’s contemplative material entered through Zen because Linehan, a praying Christian, could not find the Christian equivalent in teachable form. Some conservative readers will feel this concedes too much. It was chosen because it is true, and because it obliges Part II to actually supply the replacement rather than merely object.

Doctrinal calls

The infirmity / iniquity / injury triage

Constructed frame, flagged as such

These three categories are assembled from Scripture rather than named in it as a set. The frame is defended as pastoral triage, and chapter three carries an explicit guard against the obvious misuse — treating them as three zones of the self, with the body as morally inert territory. The guard is a red box, not a footnote.

Fatherly discipline preserved as a distinct category

Added after review

An earlier draft’s attack on “your suffering is a punishment” was strong enough to swallow Hebrews 12. Chapter three now holds both, with three guards on the discipline material — and one of them, that it cannot be used as a diagnostic, is the load-bearing one.

Gratitude as exhaust, not fuel

The trap named in front of the reader

Chapter two stops mid-argument to show why “run on gratitude instead of fear” relocates the tribunal rather than closing it. The engine is placed on Philippians 2:12–13 and the Spirit’s work, not on a feeling the reader must generate.

The cry of dereliction left unexplained

Stated, not diagrammed

Chapter nine affirms that Christ bore real God-forsakenness in his human nature and declines to say how, noting that any account which resolves it neatly has gone further than the church ever has. Deliberate restraint, modelled on Chalcedon.

Assurance located outside the believer

Christological, not introspective

Chapter eight argues that am I acceptable is not answerable by looking inward and that asking it there is the category error producing decades-long searches. 1 John 3:19–20 is marked as one of the book’s three cornerstones. Reviewers who hold a stronger doctrine of inward evidences may want to press here.

Corrections aimed inside the church

2 Corinthians 10:5 — taking every thought captive

Named as commonly misapplied

Routinely used on people with intrusive thoughts as though it were an instruction for three in the morning. In context it is polemical warfare against arguments opposing the knowledge of God. Chapter six names the honest ground instead (Philippians 4:8, Colossians 3:1–2) and tells anyone who has had it used on them that an intrusive thought is not a disobedience.

2 Corinthians 13:5 — examine yourselves

Named as commonly weaponized

Paul is writing to a church questioning his legitimacy and expects the answer yes. The command to self-examine is real; this verse is not the whip it gets used as.

The teaching that sufficient faith would have lifted the affliction

Answered with Paul’s thorn, and never by naming a tribe

Chapter three refuses the teaching without characterizing any group that holds it. The argument is that the apostle prayed specifically and repeatedly for relief and was told no.

Where the book declines to take a side

These are secondary matters on which sincere Christians differ. The book names what each side protects and does not adjudicate. A reviewer should notice the accumulation here and decide whether it is principled restraint or evasion — it is a pattern, not four isolated decisions.

Sovereignty and human responsibility

Both sides’ concerns named; no mechanism proposed

Chapter four uses this deliberately as a live demonstration of its own discipline.

Romans 7 — who is being described

Marked Contested

The simul is rested on Galatians 5:17 and 1 John 1:8–9 instead, so nothing depends on the disputed reading.

The imprecatory psalms

Marked Contested; narrow claim only

No position on whether believers may pray them as their own. The claim retained is that they model transferring vengeance to God rather than enacting it, which Romans 12:19 commands directly.

Application of the fourth commandment

No position

Chapter eleven rests only on what is not in dispute: that God legislated rest and framed it as something done for people.

Textual and lexical

Luke 22:43–44 — the sweat like drops of blood

Flagged as textually disputed; not relied on

Absent from several of the earliest manuscripts. Chapter nine builds the anguish on Mark, Matthew, and Hebrews 5:7 instead.

Barē and phortion in Galatians 6

Marked Interpretation

Chapter fifteen leans hard on the vocabulary distinction and admits that not every commentator does, some reading the difference as stylistic. The practical conclusions are shown to survive without it.

Exodus 12 — the Egyptian gold

Marked Interpretation; declared droppable

An illustration the text did not intend as a principle. The box says outright that if it does nothing for you, nothing above it falls down.

Sources and register

Brother Lawrence, a Carmelite, cited for practice

Included and named as Catholic rather than smuggled

Defended on the grounds that the practice is doctrinally unloaded and that his book being the best short treatment available is a fact about our shelves. Cleanly removable if a reviewer objects.

Heidelberg Catechism used alongside Westminster

Used without labelling it Continental

Q1 is the best available answer to the identity question. Consistent with the book’s broadly Protestant rather than strictly confessional posture.

The David Foster Wallace passage

Included, in a bordered box, turned toward the reader

Chapter sixteen names his death to make a specific argument — that insight without rescue is insufficient — and takes care to make the point about insight rather than about him. This is the single most delicate page in the book and it should have a second reader before publication.

The scrupulosity warning

Added on the writer’s initiative, not requested

Chapter seven’s practice is co-optable by religious OCD, in which Scripture and prayer become reassurance compulsions and look like unusually good discipleship from every angle. Given who this book is for, omitting it would have been negligent.

Positioning between biblical-counselling traditions

Integrationist, not replacement

The book sits with the strand that works alongside clinical care rather than the older strand that would advise leaving it. Every chapter approaching crisis refers back to the clinician by design.

Part VI — The Bruised Reed

The wing was added after the main book was complete, at the writer’s request, to address a specific kind of history. Its calls are listed separately because they carry different risks.

A separate wing rather than chapters grafted on

Own door, own contents, main sixteen untouched

Adding eight chapters after chapter sixteen would have blunted its ending and shifted the book’s genre from written for anyone toward memoir. The wing is in the main navigation but framed at its door as a second entrance, and every chapter in it stands alone because the realistic reader arrives at one page from a link.

Write to the pattern, never to the person

Hard rule, applied throughout

The wing draws on a private history and none of it appears. The test applied to every paragraph: could a reader who knows the writer reconstruct his history from this? Where the answer was yes, it was cut. The patterns addressed are common and documented, which is what makes the rule costless.

Cross-linking a companion site

Yes, with graceful degradation

My Toolbox covers the same patterns clinically. Rather than duplicate that material, the wing references it: the Toolbox names the pattern, this book works it. Links resolve through a single configurable base — unset, they render as plain named text, so an unpublished companion can never produce a broken link here.

Ch. 20 — the courtroom rule granted without qualification

Conceded fully; complaint narrowed

Institutions that handle serious offences exclude formation history because offenders deploy it to minimize and victims are injured a second time. The chapter grants that without argument and tells a reader looking for mitigation to close it. The retained complaint is narrow: the rule was written for a proceeding and migrates into treatment and pastoral settings, where there is no one left for it to protect.

Ch. 20 — the split sentence

Built in as a required practice, not a caveat

“I shouldn’t be expected to be perfect” does two jobs and only one is safe. The chapter requires the two claims to be stated as separate sentences and gives the wording. The intervention is deliberately grammatical rather than confrontational, so a client who cannot separate them finds the exercise difficult in a way that is informative.

Ch. 23 — the accountability guard

Stated in the text, not the footnotes

A changed identity removes no safeguard, restriction, or obligation. The chapter says so without qualification and models it from Acts 9, where reasonable fear of a dangerous history is recorded without criticism alongside somebody willing to vouch. A theology producing fewer safeguards for others would have refuted itself.

Ch. 23 — 1 Corinthians 6 cited without enumerating the list

Deliberate

The argument lives in the verb tense of verse 11, not in the items. Enumerating them would import a contested translation debate irrelevant to the chapter and would distract every reader who has a stake in it.

Ch. 19 — kept general rather than church-specific

Ezekiel 34 does the work; church is one instance among several

This avoids pre-empting a separate spiritual-abuse treatment still awaiting the writer’s framing decisions, and makes the chapter usable by readers whose experience was familial, institutional, or clinical rather than congregational.

Ch. 19 — the unteachability guard

Included as the third and hardest guard

The care/control frame can reclassify every correction as domination and produce a permanently unteachable stance, which would contradict chapter seventeen. The offered discriminator is outcome rather than affect: correction aims at capability, control aims at compliance.

Ch. 22 — separating forgiveness, reconciliation and trust

Three things, held apart

Only forgiveness is commanded unilaterally. Reconciliation requires repentance; trust is earned and Scripture explicitly models withholding it. The chapter also states that it is not a place to relocate one’s own ledger — a reader who comes away lighter about their own conduct has read it wrong.

Ch. 18 — failure treated as diagnostic

Stated in the chapter

If the reframe does not land — if divine omniscience still reads as threat — the chapter says that is information rather than a failure of understanding, and usually indicates a God-concept carrying a specific person’s face. Worth pursuing rather than pushing past.

Ch. 21 — Judas read primarily as a case study in worldly grief

Limitation conceded in the chapter itself

The Gospels also frame Judas theologically — Satan entering, the fulfilment language. The chapter now states outright that it is not offering a complete account of him and is not explaining that material away; it concerns only what his remorse did and did not do.

Ch. 20 — the Luke 12 extension

Conceded in the text

In its setting, much was given refers most directly to revelation and stewardship entrusted rather than to developmental formation. The chapter names that as a step beyond the immediate referent and rests the point additionally on Romans 2:12–16 rather than on Luke alone.

Ch. 23 — how far the label is overruled

Narrowed

An earlier draft said a label cannot govern what God says you are. That overreached: Scripture names acts, and frequently more exactly than institutions do. The claim now is narrower and correct — what no institution can do is make the naming final for someone who has been washed, sanctified and justified.

Still open

Items the writer flagged and has not resolved, offered to reviewers rather than settled:

  • Whether the accumulated refusals to adjudicate secondary issues amount to a coherent method or to avoidance.
  • Whether Part II’s replacement practices are genuinely sufficient to discharge chapter one’s promise, or whether the critique still outweighs what is put in the reader’s hand.
  • Whether the composite case studies are pitched at the right level of specificity — recognizable without being intrusive.
  • Whether a book with this much theological content should be handed to a secular clinician at all, or whether the Note to the Counselor is doing enough work.
  • Whether Part VI’s case studies have developed a house grievance. Several concern a person meeting an unstated standard. That is an accurate pattern and it recurs; a reviewer should say whether the repetition is faithful or narrowing.
  • Whether the wing belongs in the main navigation at all, or whether a link-only entrance would serve its readers better. It is currently in the main nav by decision, not by default.

This book is not treatment. It is written to be read alongside care from a qualified professional, not instead of it. If you are in immediate danger, contact your clinician or, in the US, call or text 988.
